# Sector Rotation & Risk Dashboard — Findings Summary

**Prepared for:** Meridian Capital Advisors, Investment Committee
**Period covered:** 3-year trailing daily data across 11 S&P 500 sector ETFs

---

## Headline Finding

Technology delivered the highest raw return in the portfolio (~119% cumulative), but Energy delivered the best *risk-adjusted* return (Sharpe ratio of 2.97 vs. Technology's 1.54) while doing so with meaningfully lower volatility (24.95% vs. 35.09% at the 90th percentile). **On a risk-adjusted basis, Energy — not Technology — is the stronger current allocation candidate, and the data supports increasing exposure to it rather than simply holding steady.**

## Supporting Evidence

**1. Return alone is misleading investors toward Technology.** Technology's cumulative return is more than double Energy's (119% vs. 56%), which would naturally draw a raw-return-only allocation process toward overweighting it. But Technology's Sharpe ratio (1.54) is less than half of Energy's (2.97) — meaning investors are paid substantially less per unit of risk taken in Technology than in Energy, despite the flashier headline number.

**2. Energy's lower volatility means more predictable outcomes.** Energy's P90 annualized volatility (24.95%) is notably below Technology's (35.09%). In practice, this means Energy's return pattern is more consistent and less prone to extreme swings — an investor has a clearer, more reliable picture of what to expect from Energy than from Technology, which helps justify sizing a larger position with more confidence.

**3. Utilities stands out as a clear underperformer on a risk-adjusted basis.** Despite reasonable volatility (19.66% P90 — among the calmer sectors in the dataset), Utilities posted the worst Sharpe ratio in the portfolio at -3.58, alongside one of the lowest cumulative returns (47%). This combination — low reward without a corresponding low-risk payoff — makes it the weakest candidate for continued allocation among the sectors reviewed.

**4. A few sectors offer a genuine "sweet spot."** Financials (Sharpe 2.11, cumulative return 77%) and Materials (Sharpe 2.14) both deliver strong risk-adjusted performance without requiring Technology-level risk exposure — worth considering as diversification anchors alongside a larger Energy position.

## Recommendation

**Increase allocation to Energy.** The combination of a leading Sharpe ratio and comparatively low volatility indicates the sector is currently compensating investors well for the risk taken, with more predictable return behavior than the portfolio's top raw-return performer (Technology). Continued or expanded exposure to Financials and Materials is also supported by their above-average Sharpe ratios. Utilities warrants a closer look for reduction, given its negative risk-adjusted return despite low volatility — the sector isn't being rewarded for the risk it does carry.

## Caveat

This analysis is based on point-in-time trailing metrics (Sharpe and volatility calculated as of the most recent 30-day window) and a 3-year lookback period. Sector performance is cyclical — Energy's current risk-adjusted strength may reflect commodity price conditions specific to this period, and should be revisited each rebalancing cycle rather than treated as a permanent allocation rule.
